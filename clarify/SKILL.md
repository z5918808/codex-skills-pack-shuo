# Clarify

Use the product voice and context from `impeccable`; this skill changes wording, not behavior or policy.

For each message identify: what happened, why it matters, what the user can do, and any consequence or timing.

- Use the user’s domain language, not internal implementation terms.
- Prefer specific action labels over “OK,” “Submit,” or “Click here.”
- Preserve legally or operationally required meaning.
- Keep terminology stable across navigation, forms, errors, and help.
- Put field errors near the field and destructive consequences before confirmation.
- Avoid blame, cute jokes during failure, vague “something went wrong,” and claims the system cannot verify.
- Consider localization, variable length, screen readers, and character constraints.

Verify copy in the actual interface, with realistic names and error states, and confirm the next action is clear without outside explanation.
